# The Keeper of Inishfall

## Chapter 1: The Lamp

The lighthouse on Inishfall had not missed a night in sixty years, and Siobhan did not intend to be the one who broke the record. She climbed the hundred and twelve steps at dusk with a tin of oil in one hand and the logbook in the other.

The wind came in from the west, the way it always did in October. It found every gap in the old stone and made the tower hum like a bottle someone was blowing across.

“You'll freeze up there,” her father had said that morning, without looking up from his nets. “Take the grey coat.” She had not taken the grey coat.

At the top, the lamp room smelled of brass polish and paraffin. She trimmed the wick, wound the clockwork, and watched the great lens begin to turn. The beam swept out over the water, caught the rocks at Carraig Dubh, and moved on.

## Chapter 2: The Boat

Just after midnight, a light answered from the sea. It was small and low, a hand lantern swinging on a boat that had no business being out on a night like this.

Siobhan wrote the time in the logbook: 00:14. Then she wrote it again, because her hand had shaken the first time and the numbers looked like someone else's.

The boat was heading for the rocks. Whoever was steering either didn't know the channel or couldn't see it, and from up here she could see both.

She did the only thing the keepers' manual allowed. She opened the shutter on the signal lamp and flashed the warning, three long and one short, over and over, until her arm ached and the lantern on the water turned away.

## Chapter 3: The Morning

The boat came into the harbour at first light. A man in a borrowed coat climbed onto the slip and asked the first person he met who had been on the light last night.

“My daughter,” said her father, and went back to his nets.

The man walked up the hill to the tower. He didn't knock. He stood at the bottom of the stairs and called up, and when Siobhan came down he shook her hand for a long time without saying anything at all.

That evening she took the grey coat.
